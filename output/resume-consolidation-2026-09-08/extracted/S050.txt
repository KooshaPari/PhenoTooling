Abstract

This progress report aims to document all work completed for the first half of the CSE 578 Data
Visualization culminating project. We aim to analyze demographic factors from the United States Census
Bureau data to develop marketing profiles for enrollment initiatives in planning at UVW College. Initial
work includes base exploration, data cleaning, and preparation of the 32k record Adult Income dataset for
comprehensive analysis and generation of intuitive visualizations.

1. Problem Statement

XYZ Corporation provides demographic marketing profiles to various organizations, including UVW
College. In partnership, XYZ aims to develop marketing profiles that supports UVW College’s
enrollment strategies by analyzing Census Bureau data to identify key characteristics distinguishing low
and high-income individuals. The analysis encompasses a variety of variables such as age, gender,
education level, marital status, and other factors. Understanding the distinctions between each enables the
development of optimal marketing efforts tailored exactly to each demographic segment. The key goal is
to develop visualizations and predictive insights that inform UVW College’s initiatives by identifying
which demographics are most likely to benefit from their various offerings.

4. Issues and Challenges Encountered

1. Missing Data Representations: The provided dataset uses “?” to represent missing values, which is not
natively handled and requires explicit logic to gracefully clean rather than automatic pandas
interpretations. Custom pre-processing logic was inserted prior to data analysis to prepare and “garden”
the data provided.

2: Education redundancies: Initially treated education and education-num as independent attributes,
despite encoding effectively identical information. Using both together can inflate attribute coverage
without adding utility for analyses.

5. Resolution Plan

1. Missing Data: Implemented explicit pre-processing logic to replace “?” with None\NaN,
(na_values=[”?”]) then applied the correct imputation or exclusion step based on variable type and
missingness patterns (dropna() for col w\ low missingness rates).

2: Education: Excluded education-num from all analyses, and retained the categorical education as it
maps perfectly to marketing relevant degree labels.
