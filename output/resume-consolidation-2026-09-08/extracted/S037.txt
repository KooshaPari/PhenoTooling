                                                               Abstract
        This final report documents the completed analysis of U.S. Census Income data to support UVW College’s marketing campaigns
    and decision frameworks. The objective of this project is to identify income-related demographic patterns and translate them into
    practical audience profiles for enrollment strategy.
        We present five user-story-driven visualizations, corresponding conclusions, project questions and implemented solutions, and
    scoped future work for the application team.


                                              I. G OALS AND B USINESS O BJECTIVE
A. Goals
  1) Identify demographic and work-related attributes most associated with income label (≤ 50K vs. > 50K).
  2) Build clear, readable visual evidence for at least five prioritized user stories.
  3) Convert findings into practical marketing guidance for UVW College implementation teams.

B. Business Objective
   Provide UVW College with demographic targeting guidance so marketing resources can be focused on the segments most
likely to benefit from degree completion and career advancement pathways provided.

                                                         II. A SSUMPTIONS
A. Technical Assumptions
  1) After cleaning missing markers (?), the Adult dataset is valid for directional analysis.
  2) The income label split (≤ 50K vs. > 50K) is suitable for segmentation decisions.
  3) Patterns observed are informative for campaign strategy, even if not properly causal.

B. Business Assumptions
  1) Income mobility is a strong enrollment motivator for prospective students.
  2) Better demographic segmentation improves overall campaign effectiveness.
  3) Different demographic segments require different message framing.
  4) UVW teams can utilize these insights in campaign and application design.

                                                  IV. V ISUALIZATIONS
A. Visualization 1: Age Distribution by Income (Univariate)




Fig. 1. Age distribution by income class.

  What it demonstrates: Higher-income records are concentrated in older age ranges compared to lower-income records.
Why selected: Histogram comparison is the most direct method for distribution differences by class.
Design process: Distribution choice → bin calibration → class color consistency → readability tuning.
Conclusion: UVW can separate early-career and mid-career outreach narratives based on age-income patterns.

B. Visualization 2: Education Level vs Income (Univariate)




Fig. 2. Education level and income composition.

  What it demonstrates: Income composition shifts toward > 50K as educational attainment increases.
Why selected: Normalized stacked horizontal bars compare category composition clearly.
Design process: Category ordering → normalization → label/legend refinement → readability checks.
Conclusion: Degree-completion messaging is especially relevant for some-college and associate-level segments.

C. Visualization 3: Age, Education-Num, and Hours-Per-Week vs Income (Multivariate)




Fig. 3. Multivariate view of age, weekly hours, education years, and income class.

  What it demonstrates: Income class separation improves when age, work hours, and education years are considered together.
Why selected: Scatter with multi-encoding (position, size, color) preserves three explanatory factors.
Design process: Balanced sampling → encoding selection → overlap control (alpha/size) → legend optimization.
Conclusion: Flexible program formats should be emphasized for working adults with constrained weekly hours.

                                 V. Q UESTIONS R AISED AND I MPLEMENTED S OLUTIONS
  1) Question: How can class imbalance distort visual interpretation?
     Implemented solution: Used normalized proportions and balanced sampling where needed.
  2) Question: Which attribute combinations are most useful for UVW decisions?
     Implemented solution: Prioritized combinations tied to actionable marketing decisions instead of exhaustive pairing.
  3) Question: How can multivariate analysis remain readable?
     Implemented solution: Used constrained encodings, reduced clutter, and category filtering for interpretability.
  4) Question: How should sparse variables (for example, capital-gain) be handled?
     Implemented solution: Used targeted views to isolate informative non-zero behavior while retaining context.

                                              VII. P ROFESSIONAL S UMMARY
   All in all, project provides complete, well-aligned analyses outputs suitable for UVW’s incoming marketing strategy. The
analysis confirms that income-related differences are best explained by combinations of demographic, education, and work-
context factors rather than any single attribute. The five user-story visualizations provide implementation-ready guidance for
UVW’s marketing application: who to prioritize, what message to deliver, and how to tailor campaigns by segment. In practical
terms, UVW should focus on high-opportunity groups (<=50K, working adults, degree-incomplete learners) with flexible
program positioning and clear income-mobility value propositions, while maintaining inclusive outreach across demographic
groups.
